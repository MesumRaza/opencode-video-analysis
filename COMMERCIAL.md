# Commercial licensing

`opencode-video-analysis` is released under **AGPL-3.0-or-later**. That
licence is free for individuals, education, research, open-source projects and
internal evaluation. It is *not* free for a business that wants to keep its
source closed, because the AGPL requires any distributed or network-served
derivative to publish its Corresponding Source under the same terms.

A **commercial licence** from the author removes those obligations and lets you
use this code inside a closed product or paid service.

## You need a commercial licence if

- you ship this code (or a derivative) inside a **closed-source** product, SDK,
  appliance or on-premise package;
- you host it as part of a **paid or commercially operated service** and do not
  intend to publish the service's source under AGPL-3.0;
- you redistribute it as part of a **commercial bundle** without disclosing
  source;
- your legal or procurement policy forbids AGPL-3.0 software.

## You do **not** need one if

- you use it personally, in teaching, research, or a noncommercial project;
- you use it internally at a company for evaluation or non-revenue purposes;
- your product or service is itself released under AGPL-3.0-or-later and you
  meet its obligations (source offer, notices, modification disclosure);
- you only want to read, fork and experiment with it.

If you are unsure, ask before you ship. The AGPL is strict about network use,
and a mistake is expensive to unwind.

## What a commercial licence gives you

- use in closed-source commercial products and hosted services
- no obligation to publish your modifications
- the right to redistribute under your own terms
- written confirmation of the above for procurement or legal review

Support, indemnity or a source-code escrow can be negotiated as part of the
agreement. Pricing depends on the scale of use.

## How to request one

Open an issue titled **“Commercial license request”** on
<https://github.com/mesumraza/opencode-video-analysis/issues> (or contact the
author through that GitHub profile) and describe:

1. what you want to build with it,
2. whether you will redistribute it or only host it,
3. roughly how many users or installs you expect.

Nothing in this document is itself a licence, an offer, or legal advice; the
signed commercial agreement is the licence.
